Chapter 1: Echoes of Care and Love

The night had settled in like a thick, suffocating blanket. Outside the cabin, the world was swallowed whole by darkness. The sky above was a smothering shade of black, heavy clouds blocking out any trace of the moon, leaving only the faintest glimmer of distant stars that seemed too far away to matter. The snow that blanketed the ground reflected none of the warmth that it might have under a brighter sky. Instead, it lay still and lifeless, a frozen sea stretching endlessly in every direction. The trees outside groaned in protest, their skeletal branches creaking with the strain of the wind. They stood like ancient sentinels, gnarled and twisted by years of fighting the storm, their branches weighed down by thick frost that glistened in the faint light. Their dark silhouettes against the black sky looked almost like ghosts, moving, shifting, as though they were trying to escape the wind’s relentless assault.

In the midst of the endless white desert, the night stretched on in its frozen silence, with nothing but the howling wind to disturb the stillness. Every light in every house had been extinguished, as though the cold had consumed them all, leaving only the ghostly outlines of homes standing in the vast expanse. The snow clung to every surface, making the landscape appear deserted, untouched, as if the world itself had frozen in time. But in one small house, nestled against the storm, a single light flickered through the window, a small beacon in the overwhelming darkness. Inside, the fire crackled in the hearth, its warm glow casting long shadows across the room. The room was still, save for the soft, steady voice of a father speaking to his daughter. "Tell me a story, Daddy," the young girl, Mouni, whispered from her place by the fire. Her wide eyes, full of innocence and curiosity, gazed up at him, waiting for the comfort of a tale to chase away the shadows of the night. "You want to hear a story?" he murmured, his voice quiet but steady. "All right... I will tell you a story of a man who fought for a person whom you loved more". Mouni sat up straighter, her eyes wide as she listened intently to the tale unfolding before her. Rolex’s voice had grown heavier, his words weighed down by memories long buried. “…And then, after the battle was lost, after Koshima had given everything to stop him… Peter killed Koshima.” The sentence hung in the air like a thunderclap, cutting through the silence of the room. Mouni blinked, her small face filled with confusion. “What…? Peter? But… why?” “Enough of this for tonight, Mouni. It is late. Go to sleep.”

Mouni looked up at him, her brow furrowed, but she nodded slowly, sensing the change in his tone. She had heard this before. Stories of the past that still stung, stories that had no answers. “Goodnight, Daddy,” she whispered, tucking herself into the blanket and closing her eyes, though her mind still swirled with questions. Rolex sat quietly for a moment longer, staring into the fire, the crackling flames a constant reminder of a world that seemed so far away from the one he now inhabited. “Goodnight, Mouni,” he whispered back, his voice barely audible and he slept in front of her legs.

The morning was a thick haze of gray, the fog outside so dense it seemed to cling to the windows, making the world beyond the house appear as though it were wrapped in a shroud. Rolex stirred in his bed, his body heavy with the weight of restless sleep. The muffled cries of the wind from the night before had softened into an eerie stillness, broken only by the distant caw of a bird cutting through the fog.

The shrill ring of his phone jolted him fully awake. With a groggy sigh, Rolex fumbled on the bedside table, his fingers brushing past a half-empty glass of water before landing on the device. The screen’s dim glow revealed a name he had not expected to see this early: Dave.

“Hello?” Rolex’s voice was hoarse, his tone betraying his lingering exhaustion.

“Rolex, my friend!” Dave’s voice was warm but carried an unmistakable urgency. “I hope I didn’t wake you.”

“You did,” Rolex admitted, rubbing his face, trying to shake the last remnants of sleep. “What’s the rush?”

There was a pause on the other end, followed by a familiar sigh. “Look, I know you are holding out for a proper chef’s role—I get it, you deserve that—but I have got something for you today. It is a kitchen porter position. Not glamorous, I know, but it will keep you busy.”

Rolex sat up, the weight of Dave’s words settling over him. He had been waiting for weeks, trusting Dave’s promise to find him a position worthy of his skills. Kitchen porter was not what he had envisioned—it was far from the dream.

“Dave,” he started, his tone sharp with frustration, but the recruiter-turned-chef interrupted.

“Listen, mate, I know it’s not ideal,” Dave said, his voice softening. “But hear me out. I am keeping my ear to the ground for a proper chef role. You have my word on that. This is just… something to tide you over. What do you say?”

Rolex leaned back against the headboard, staring out at the fog pressing against the window. His thoughts were a tangle of pride and practicality. Finally, he exhaled, long and slow.

“Alright,” he said, his voice tinged with resignation. “I’ll take it.”

“Good man,” Dave replied, relief evident in his tone. “I will send you the details. Just hang in there, Rolex. I will not let you down.”

As the call ended, Rolex sat there in the silence of the room, the weight of his decision heavy on his shoulders. Outside, the fog remained unmoving, as if the world itself were unsure of what lay ahead. But one thing was certain: he had taken the first step, however small, on a path he had not planned for.

The morning fog pressed thickly against the windows, muting the weak light that struggled to filter through. Inside the room, warmth lingered from the night, the faint crackle of the radiator the only sound apart from the soft rhythm of Mouni’s breathing.

Rolex sat on the edge of the bed, his phone call with Dave still fresh in his mind. He ran a hand through his hair, glancing over at his daughter, who lay curled up beneath her blanket, her tiny form barely moving. For a moment, he hesitated, not wanting to disturb her peace, but time was slipping away.

“Mouni,” he said softly, leaning closer to the bed. “Come on, sweetheart, time to wake up.”

A muffled groan came from under the blanket. She did not move, except to burrow further into the covers.

“Mouni,” he called again, this time gently patting her back. “It is morning. You need to get ready for school.”

Finally, her head peeked out, her hair a tangled mess and her face scrunched in half-sleep annoyance. “Five more minutes, Dad,” she mumbled.

Rolex chuckled. “You said that yesterday, and yesterday turned into ‘I’m late for school.’ Come on now.”

Her eyes opened a little wider as her mind began to catch up. She sat up slowly, her arms stretching high above her head. Then, as if remembering something important, she turned to him.

“Daddy,” she said, her voice still groggy but hopeful, “are we going to the exhibition today?”

Rolex hesitated, his heart sinking at the expectation in her tone. He leaned closer, his expression soft but serious. “Not today, Mouni. Daddy has to work. But I promise, we will go soon. As soon as I have a free day, I will take you. That is a promise.”

Her face fell briefly, disappointment flashing across her features, but she gave a small nod. “Okay,” she whispered.

“Good girl,” he said, ruffling her hair lightly. “Now, up you go. Get ready for school. Uncle John will pick you up after.”

She slumped back onto the pillow. “School is so boring,” she groaned. “Why can’t I just stay in bed like a lion?”

“Lions don’t stay in bed,” Rolex replied with a grin. “And speaking of lions, they need to take baths, too. So off to the bathroom.”

Mouni shot him a mischievous look, her lips curling into a sly smile. “Lions don’t take baths.”

Rolex leaned back, crossing his arms with a raised eyebrow. “Well, you are not a lion, Mouni. You are a little girl, and little girls take baths.”

Her grin widened. “But, Dad, I am saving water by not bathing. It is good for the planet.”

Rolex laughed, shaking his head. “Saving the planet can wait. Right now, you are saving yourself from smelling like a lion’s den. Off you go.”

With an exaggerated sigh and a reluctant shuffle, she slid out of bed and made her way to the bathroom, her humor not dimmed in the slightest. Rolex watched her go, smiling despite the heaviness in his chest. No matter how challenging his days became, Mouni’s sharp wit and boundless spirit always found a way to make the weight a little easier to bear.

Mouni, freshly bathed and bundled up in her coat, skipped lightly beside her father, her small backpack bouncing with every step. Rolex watched her, smiling softly. She seemed so carefree, her energy lighting up the gray morning.

When they arrived at the school gates, the faint sound of children’s laughter and chatter filled the air. Mouni turned to him, her bright eyes scanning his face.

“Dear, I’m leaving now,” Rolex said gently, crouching to meet her eye level. “Uncle John will pick you up in the evening, alright?”

“Ok, Dad,” she replied, giving him a quick hug. As she pulled away, her gaze darted to the group of children running toward the gate. Her face lit up. “Bye, Dad! My friends are coming!”

Before Rolex could respond, she was off, racing toward her friends with the kind of enthusiasm only children could muster. She called out to them, her voice ringing with laughter as they welcomed her into their group.

Rolex stood frozen by the gate, his heart catching in his chest as he watched her disappear into the crowd of children. A lump formed in his throat, and without warning, tears pricked his eyes. He wiped them quickly, looking around as if someone might notice.

It was a strange feeling—bittersweet, tangled, and overwhelming. He felt a pang of sadness, watching her run off without a backward glance, as if the bond they shared had been momentarily overshadowed by the pull of her friends. But at the same time, he felt a swell of pride and joy. She was growing up, finding her place in the world, and forming connections that would shape her future.

For a moment, he just stood there, staring at the schoolyard where his daughter was now laughing with her friends. His breath clouded in the cold air as he exhaled slowly, the weight of mixed emotions settling on his shoulders.

“She’s growing up,” he murmured to himself, a bittersweet smile touching his lips.

With one last glance, he turned away, the school gate closing behind him. As he walked back into the foggy morning, the sound of her laughter lingered in his ears—a reminder of the fleeting, beautiful moments that made being her father the most rewarding and heart-wrenching thing he had ever known.

The fog was beginning to thin as Rolex drove through the quiet streets, his car’s headlights cutting through the lingering gray. The morning air felt heavier than usual, weighed down by thoughts of Mouni and the long day ahead. He tightened his grip on the steering wheel, focusing on the road as the familiar ache of responsibility settled in his chest.

Up ahead, a figure stood at the side of the road, waving urgently. Rolex slowed, squinting through the mist until the outline of a woman became clear. She looked young, bundled in a coat, her breath visible in the cold air as she waved again.

Rolex rolled down the window as he pulled up beside her. “Everything alright?”

The woman stepped closer, her cheeks flushed from the chill. “Oh, thank you for stopping,” she said, her voice tinged with relief. “I missed my bus, and the next one is not for an hour. I am going to be late for work. Could you give me a lift? It is just to The Stables Restaurant.”

Rolex raised an eyebrow, surprised by the coincidence. “The Stables? I am heading there myself.”

Her face brightened. “Really? That is perfect! I work as a waitress there. I am Emma Wilson,” she said, extending her hand through the open window. “Thanks so much for stopping.”

He hesitated for only a moment before nodding. “Alright, hop in.”

Emma climbed into the passenger seat, rubbing her hands together to warm them. Rolex glanced at her briefly as he started driving again. She seemed friendly, her energy a stark contrast to the subdued morning.

“So, you’re new at The Stables?” Emma asked after a moment, breaking the silence.

“Sort of,” Rolex replied. “I am starting today. Kitchen porter.”

Emma gave him a curious look. “Oh, that is great! You are going to like it there. Well… as much as anyone can like restaurant work,” she added with a wry smile. “It can get hectic, but the team’s good. Have you worked in kitchens?”

“More than I’d care to admit,” Rolex said, his lips curving into a faint smile. “Let’s just say it’s not my first rodeo.”

Emma laughed, her breath fogging the window. “Well, that is good to hear. You will fit right in, I’m sure. And thanks again for the ride. Missing the bus really threw me off.”

“No problem,” Rolex said, his eyes on the road. “Everyone needs a little help sometimes.”

As they continued toward the restaurant, their conversation flowed easily, Emma’s upbeat chatter contrasting with Rolex’s quieter demeanor. For a brief moment, the weight of the morning lifted, replaced by the simple comfort of a shared destination.

By the time they pulled into the parking lot of The Stables, the fog had begun to clear, revealing the building’s rustic facade. Rolex glanced at Emma as she gathered her things. “Thanks again,” she said, smiling warmly. “See you inside.”

“See you,” he replied, watching as she walked toward the entrance.

Rolex sat in the car for a moment longer, taking a deep breath before stepping into what he knew would be a long day. Meeting Emma had been an unexpected start to the morning, but it was a small reminder that even on the foggiest days, connections could still be made.

Rolex walked into the manager’s office, his nerves steady despite the newness of the place. Mrs. Carter, the restaurant manager, glanced up from her paperwork as he approached.

“Dave sent me,” Rolex said simply. “I’m here for the kitchen porter job.”

Mrs. Carter tilted her head in acknowledgment and picked up the phone. “Ah, yes. He mentioned you would be starting today. Welcome. I will call Mark, the head chef, to get you settled in.”

Moments later, Mark strode in, his tall frame and commanding presence immediately noticeable. “You’re the new porter?” he asked, shaking Rolex’s hand firmly.

“That’s me,” Rolex replied.

“Alright,” Mark said, leading him out. “Let us get you into the kitchen and meet the team. It is going to be a busy day.”

The kitchen was a storm of activity, but Mark guided Rolex through it smoothly, introducing him to the staff and walking him through the tasks. By the time evening rolled around, Rolex had found his rhythm, working efficiently to keep the kitchen running smoothly.

As the dinner rush ended and the staff began tidying up, Emma approached Rolex at the sink where he was scrubbing a stack of pots. She leaned casually against the counter, a mischievous smile playing on her lips.

“So,” she began, “what time do you usually head home?”

Rolex wiped his hands on a towel and glanced at the clock. “Depends on when we wrap up, but usually between 10:30 and 11:30.”

Emma raised an eyebrow. “That late? No wonder I have never seen you leave before me.” She hesitated for a moment before adding, “Hey, could you do me a favor? I end up spending thirty pounds every night just to get home. If you are heading the same way, maybe you could drop me off? I will pay you.”

Rolex thought for a moment, then nodded. “Sure, that works. Save us both some trouble.”

Emma’s grin widened. “Great. Thanks, Rolex. You are a lifesaver.”

When the shift finally ended, Rolex and Emma walked out together. She slid into the passenger seat of his car, her movements brisk against the biting cold. Rolex started the engine, the rumble breaking the quiet of the night.

As they drove through the dark streets, the tension of the day began to fade, replaced by lighthearted conversation.

“You know,” Emma teased, “you’ve got this serious vibe going on, but I bet you secretly love cracking jokes in your downtime.”

Rolex smirked, glancing at her. “Maybe. But only when they are good jokes, unlike yours.”

She gasped dramatically, clutching her chest. “How dare you! My humor is top-tier.”

“Sure,” he said with a chuckle. “Top-tier cringe.”

They both burst out laughing, the sound filling the car as the streets blurred by. The banter continued, each playful jab and shared laugh making the ride seem shorter than it was.

By the time they reached Emma’s place, the heaviness of the day had melted away, replaced by the warmth of an unexpected camaraderie.

“Thanks for the ride, Rolex,” she said as she stepped out of the car, still smiling. “See you tomorrow?”

“Yeah, see you,” he replied.

As he drove away, Rolex realized the night had not been so bad after all.

Chapter 2: Mouni’s Day

The evening air was crisp as Rolex pulled into the driveway of John’s house. The headlights from his car cut through the soft fog that rolled in from the nearby hills. Mouni, his bright-eyed daughter, was already waiting on the front steps, her small figure framed against the warm glow of the porch light. As soon as she saw him, her face lit up in excitement, and she rushed over to the car.

“Daddy!” she exclaimed, her voice bubbling with energy.

“Hey there, little one,” Rolex said, smiling as he opened the car door for her. As she hopped in and buckled herself into the passenger seat, Rolex started the engine. The hum of the car engine was a familiar comfort. The drive home was usually filled with quiet moments, but tonight was different.

“Dad, guess what?” Mouni said as they drove off, looking over at him with a mischievous smile. “Did you get a new girlfriend?”

Rolex blinked, caught off guard by her question. He shot her a glance, trying to maintain a neutral expression. “No, Mouni. Emma’s just a friend,” he replied, his voice calm but guarded.

Mouni’s smile only grew wider, her playful nature coming out. “No, no, Dad. I do not think so. I think you and her would make a good couple. I will make you two one!” she declared confidently, as though it was the most obvious thing in the world.

Rolex’s heart skipped a beat at her words, but he tried to keep his cool. He had no idea what Mouni was really thinking or how much of her words were just innocent chatter, but hearing her talk so openly about his personal life felt… strange. “You’re still a little girl, Mouni,” he said, his voice becoming more stern. “You should not talk about things like that. You are too immature to understand.”

Mouni’s face fell instantly, her eyes turning dark with anger. She crossed her arms tightly over her chest, her small body brimming with frustration. “I don’t want to talk to you tonight!” she snapped, her tone sharp and cold.

Rolex, taken aback by her sudden outburst, sighed deeply, rubbing his temples as the weight of the conversation lingered in the air. He was not prepared for this kind of confrontation, especially not with his daughter. But he knew that his words had hurt her, even if he did not fully understand why.

The car ride back was silent, save for the sound of the tires crunching on the gravel road. When they finally arrived home, Mouni did not say a word as she got out of the car. She stormed straight into the house and into her room, slamming the door behind her.

Rolex stood for a moment in the doorway, feeling the weight of the tension between them. He hated this—hated the silence that had settled between them. But he knew he had to fix it.

After a few minutes of pacing, Rolex found himself in the kitchen. He started making Mouni’s favorite meal—pasta with penny and marinara sauce. He moved about the kitchen in a quiet, almost mechanical way, his mind racing over the earlier conversation. He had never meant to upset her, but sometimes, things just came out wrong.

As the food cooked, the familiar smell of pasta sauce filled the air, a comforting aroma that usually made Mouni’s face light up. Rolex hoped it would do the trick tonight too.

A few minutes later, the door to Mouni’s room creaked open. She stepped out, still looking cross, but the smell of food seemed to coax her out of her corner. She did not say anything at first, but her eyes flicked to the table, where a bowl of warm penny awaited her.

Rolex set the table and motioned for her to sit. He could not help but mimic her earlier tone, as he gestured dramatically to the meal. “Well, I made your favorite,” he said, trying to lighten the mood with a bit of humor.

Mouni remained silent, but her eyes softened. She sat down slowly, still wrapped in her hurt feelings, but the food in front of her was hard to ignore.

Rolex took a deep breath, his voice quiet but sincere. “I’m sorry if I upset you earlier, Mouni,” he said. “You know, sometimes I say things I do not mean. I should not have shouted.”

Mouni, who had been picking at the pasta, stopped and looked up at him. Her small face softened a little, but there was still a hint of defiance in her eyes. After a long silence, she stood up suddenly, walked over to him, and gave him a playful tap on his shoulder.

“Promise you won’t shout at me again?” she asked, her voice softer now, but still with a hint of mischief.

Rolex laughed, ruffling her hair lightly. “I promise,” he said, his tone gentle and full of love. “No more shouting.”

Mouni grinned, satisfied with his answer. She returned to her seat and began to eat, her mood clearly lifting with every bite. The tension between them dissolved as the food worked its magic, and before long, Mouni was back to her usual bubbly self, chatting away about school and her day.

As the evening passed and Mouni finished her meal, she began to yawn, her little body beginning to show signs of exhaustion. Rolex smiled as she climbed into bed, her face peaceful once again.

“Goodnight, Daddy,” Mouni said sleepily, snuggling under her blanket.

“Goodnight, Mouni,” Rolex replied softly, kissing her forehead. “I love you.”

“I love you too,” she murmured, her eyes closing as she drifted off to sleep.

Rolex stood there for a moment, watching her sleep. The day had been full of challenges—emotional moments, misunderstandings—but seeing his daughter at peace once more made everything worth it.

It was clear to him that their bond would always remain unbreakable no matter what life threw at them. They would have their moments of frustration and disagreement, but in the end, love would always win out.

And that was all that truly mattered.

The sun was shining brightly, casting a warm, golden glow over the neighborhood as Rolex drove toward Emma’s apartment. Today felt different—he was not just going out for a casual day; it was a chance to bring Mouni’s world to life and share that with Emma. Rolex had promised Mouni that they would attend a child-focused exhibition, and he was excited to keep that promise. He also thought it would be a good opportunity to spend more time with Emma, and perhaps get to know her a little better.

When he arrived, Emma was already waiting outside, a soft smile on her face. She climbed into the car, greeting him with warmth.

“Hey, Rolex! How is it going?” she asked, settling into the passenger seat.

“I am good, thanks for asking. How about you?” he responded, starting the engine.

“Busy, as usual. But I am actually off tomorrow,” Emma replied, glancing out the window. “Guess I’ll be pretty bored, huh?”

Rolex grinned, an idea coming to mind. “Bored, huh? I cannot let that happen.” He turned to her with a mischievous smile. “You see, I promised Mouni I’d take her to a child-focused exhibition tomorrow. She loves those kinds of things. I thought you might want to join us.”

Emma blinked in surprise. “A child’s exhibition? That sounds fun, but… I did not know you had a daughter.”

Rolex chuckled softly. “Yeah, I know—I don’t exactly look like a dad, do I?” He smiled. “But I have a seven-year-old. Her name’s Mouni, and she’s my world. She is really excited about this exhibition. I thought it would be fun if you joined us.”

Emma paused, processing the information. “Wow, I had no idea. I would love to join you both. I am sure it will be a fun day out.”

Rolex's eyes lit up with relief. “Great! I think Mouni will be thrilled. It has been a while since we have had a full day out like this.”

As they chatted more about the event, Emma could not help but feel a little curiosity about Rolex. A man with a young daughter, planning a day out to inspire creativity—there was something endearing about him. She was also starting to realize how much she appreciated his kindness and the way he spoke about Mouni. It was clear that she meant the world to him.

That night, as the stars twinkled above, Rolex and Mouni sat together in the living room after dinner. The day had been full of excitement, but tomorrow promised even more fun.

"Mouni," Rolex said, a playful smile on his face as he ruffled her hair, "tomorrow we're going to the exhibition again. And guess what? I have a little surprise for you."

Mouni’s eyes lit up, her curiosity piqued. She looked up at her dad, her face full of expectation. "A surprise? What is it, Dad? Tell me, please!" she asked eagerly, her voice filled with excitement.

Rolex chuckled, trying to hold back the secret. "I can’t tell you just yet. It will be a surprise," he teased.

"No, Dad!" Mouni pouted, her lower lip sticking out as she pulled on his sleeve. "Please! What is the surprise?" she pressed, her voice full of determination.

Rolex held her gaze, his eyes twinkling. "Okay, okay," he said with a grin. "You really want to know?"

Mouni nodded furiously, bouncing up and down with anticipation. "Yes, yes, I do!"

“Well then," he said, lowering his voice for dramatic effect, "the surprise is... Emma will be there tomorrow at the exhibition."

Mouni’s eyes widened, and her face broke into a huge grin. “Emma?” she exclaimed, practically jumping up with excitement. “Emma is coming?!"

Rolex laughed, happy to see her so excited. "Yep, Emma will be there with us. She’s coming to spend the day at the exhibition too."

Mouni was beyond thrilled. She had liked Emma from the very first moment when Rolex said about her, and the idea of spending time with her at the exhibition made her heart swell with joy. “I cannot wait! I am going to see Emma tomorrow! Yay!” she cheered, clapping her hands together.

Rolex smiled as he watched his daughter’s excitement. “I am glad you are happy, Mouni. Now, get some rest. We have an adventure ahead of us tomorrow, and I’m sure you’ll want to be at your best.”

Mouni’s eyes sparkled with anticipation, but she nodded, her excitement still bubbling over. “Okay, Dad. I am going to bed now. Tomorrow, I will see Emma at the exhibition! I am so excited!”

As she climbed into bed, Mouni snuggled under the covers, her mind racing with thoughts of tomorrow. The idea of seeing Emma again filled her with happiness, and she could not wait to show her all the art she would make.

Before she closed her eyes, Mouni whispered to herself, “Tomorrow’s going to be so much fun.”

With that, she drifted into a peaceful sleep, dreaming of all the adventures the next day would bring.

The evening sun had started to cast a warm, golden glow over the city as Rolex and Mouni prepared for their visit to the exhibition. It had been a day filled with excitement, and now the final moments were approaching.

Mouni, eager for the evening out, had picked out a special dress for the occasion. She stood before the mirror, holding up two dresses, trying to decide. "Dad, which one looks better?" she asked, twirling around in a bright pink dress that shimmered with each movement. "This one or the yellow one?"

Rolex, looking over his daughter’s choices, could not help but smile. "Both look wonderful, Mouni, but I think the pink one is perfect for today. It’s vibrant and cheerful, just like you."

Mouni’s face lit up as she beamed. "You’re right! I will wear this one!"

After she dressed, Mouni turned to a small box in her hand. Inside were two cute hairbands—a blue one with little flowers, and a pink one with a bow. "Which one should I wear, Dad?" she asked, holding them both out for him to see.

Rolex paused, considering her request with a smile. "The blue one would look great with your dress. It’ll add just the right touch of charm."

Mouni eagerly put on the blue hairband, checking herself in the mirror once more. She was pleased with how it all came together. "Thank you, Dad! I am ready!"

As Rolex finished getting ready himself, his phone rang. He looked at the screen, his face softening as he saw the message. "Emma’s here," he said, looking up at Mouni. "She’s already at the exhibition. It looks like it’s time to head out."

Mouni bounced on her toes, her excitement obvious. "Yay, Dad! Let us go now! I cannot wait to see Emma!"

But just as Rolex grabbed his keys to the car, Mouni looked up at him with a thoughtful expression. "Dad," she began, her voice small but sincere, "can we walk to the exhibition tonight? I know you usually drive, but I really want to walk and enjoy the evening breeze. It would be so much fun!"

Rolex was momentarily surprised by the request. He had been planning to drive, but when he looked at Mouni’s hopeful face, he felt a wave of warmth. "You know what, Mouni?" he said with a smile. "That’s a wonderful idea. We’ll walk there tonight, enjoy the fresh air, and talk along the way."

Mouni’s face lit up with happiness, and she gave him a tight hug. "Thank you, Dad! It is going to be the best evening ever!"

Hand in hand, they stepped out into the evening. The sky had deepened into a soft twilight, and the streets were filled with the gentle hum of city life. People were finishing their day, and the streets were lined with lights that flickered in the growing dark. It was a peaceful and perfect setting for a walk.

As they walked, Mouni chattered excitedly about the exhibition. "I can’t wait to see Emma! And maybe she will even be waiting to see me!"

Rolex nodded, walking beside her and enjoying the moment. "I’m sure she’ll love the exhibition, Mouni. And I’m happy we decided to walk—it’s nice to slow down and enjoy each other’s company."

As they approached the exhibition venue, the lights outside gleamed brightly, signaling the start of a new adventure. Rolex glanced at Mouni, who was practically bouncing with excitement. "It’s been a beautiful walk," he said. "Sometimes, the journey is just as special as the destination."

Mouni smiled up at him. "I’m glad we walked too, Dad. I think today is going to be so much fun!"

With that, they entered the exhibition, ready for the surprises and adventures that awaited them. The evening was just beginning, and it was bound to be a night full of joy and new memories.

Chapter 3: A chance of change

The exhibition was everything Mouni had hoped for and more. She was bubbling with excitement, her eyes wide with wonder as she marveled at the vibrant colors and creative displays around her. But what made the evening even more special was the presence of Emma. Mouni could not wait to meet her, and when she finally did, her joy was evident.

"Emma, I’m so glad you’re here!" Mouni exclaimed, rushing over to greet her with a big smile. The two girls immediately hit it off, chatting about the artwork on display and sharing their thoughts about the pieces they liked best. Rolex watched the two interact with a warm smile on his face, seeing how happy Mouni was in that moment.

After hours spent wandering around, looking at the exhibits, talking, and laughing, it was finally time to leave. As they stepped outside, Mouni suddenly stopped in her tracks and tugged at her dad’s sleeve.

"Dad, I’m so hungry!" she said with a pout, her stomach rumbling. "Can we go to a restaurant? I want to eat something special tonight!"

Rolex smiled at her, but inside, he felt a pang of worry. It had been a wonderful evening, but now, with the mention of food, he realized he had to check his bank balance. He quickly pulled out his phone and glanced at his account. The numbers on the screen made his heart sink—there was not enough to cover a fancy meal for three at a restaurant.

"Don’t worry, Mouni," Rolex said, masking his concern with a calm tone. "We’ll get something to eat, but let me just make a quick call."

He pulled out his contract list from his phone, quickly scanning the contacts. His finger hovered over a name that stood out—Sr. Rolex, also known as Koshima Rolex. They had worked together on various projects before, and Rolex knew he could count on him for a bit of help in tight situations.

Rolex stepped aside for a moment and dialed Koshima's number, waiting for the call to connect. Emma, noticing her dad’s preoccupation, gently squeezed Mouni’s hand. "It’s okay, Mouni," Emma reassured her with a smile. "Your dad will take care of everything."

On the other end of the line, Koshima’s familiar voice answered. "Hello, Rolex! What is going on?"

Rolex quickly explained his situation. "Koshima, I’m in a bit of a bind. I am out with my daughter, and she is hungry. We were planning to go to a restaurant, but I have run a little low on funds. Can you send me some money?"

There was a brief silence on the other end, followed by Koshima's laughter. "You should have told me earlier, Rolex. Do not worry, I will take care of it. You’ve always been there when I needed you."

Within moments, Koshima transferred the money to Rolex’s account, and Rolex felt a sense of relief wash over him. "Thank you, Koshima."

With the situation sorted, Rolex turned to Mouni and Emma, a smile spreading across his face. "Alright, Mouni, Emma, we’re all set. How about we go somewhere special for dinner?"

Mouni’s face lit up. "Yay! What kind of restaurant are we going to, Dad?"

Rolex grinned. "How about we go to a five-star restaurant? I think we deserve a little treat after such a wonderful evening."

Excited by the prospect of a fancy meal, Mouni and Emma both eagerly agreed. They headed to a luxurious five-star restaurant, where they were greeted by the elegant ambiance and soothing music. The staff escorted them to their table, where the sparkling lights and exquisite décor made the evening feel even more magical.

As they sat down, the waiter handed them the menus, and Mouni, feeling fancy in her pink dress, picked up her menu with an excited gleam in her eye. "Dad, can I have the pasta? Please?" she asked, knowing how much she loved it.

Rolex nodded with a smile. "Of course, Mouni. You can have whatever you want."

The meal was nothing short of delicious, and the atmosphere was perfect for a memorable family evening. Rolex watched as Mouni and Emma laughed and talked about their favorite things. He felt grateful for the kindness of Koshima and was relieved to know that his daughter’s happiness was not interrupted by worries.

As the evening drew to a close, Rolex reflected on how lucky he was to have such amazing people in his life—his daughter, his friend Emma, and even the people who came to his aid when he needed them. It was a night full of surprises, laughter, and the kind of memories that would stay with him forever

As the night came to a close, after a wonderful meal and a heartwarming time spent together, Rolex looked at Mouni and Emma with a smile. The evening had been perfect—a mixture of laughter, art, food, and good company. He wanted to capture the moment, so it would stay with them forever.

"Hey Emma, could you do me a favor?" Rolex asked, his voice light but full of affection. "Could you take a selfie with Mouni and me? I’d love to remember tonight."

Emma, always happy to oblige, grinned and pulled out her phone. "Of course, Rolex! This will be fun."

Mouni beamed, standing next to her dad, eager to have a keepsake from the special day. Emma snapped a few pictures, ensuring the smiles were bright and the background of the fancy restaurant added a touch of elegance to the memory.

As the camera clicked, Rolex felt a warmth in his chest. The selfie was not just a photo; it was a symbol of the bond they shared—the love, the laughter, and the small moments that created lasting memories.

After Emma showed them the pictures, Mouni looked at the screen and squealed with joy. "I love it, Dad! We look so happy!" she said, wrapping her arms around Rolex.

Emma smiled softly, her heart full from the joy she saw between them. "You both look wonderful. It’s a memory to treasure forever."

With the photo safely stored in their phones, Rolex glanced at Mouni and then at Emma. The evening had been filled with so much love and happiness, and now, they had a perfect picture to remember it all.

As the evening began to wind down, they were all ready to head home. The night had been full of fun, and they were feeling content. However, when they stepped outside the 5-star restaurant, Emma tried to book a cab to return home. She tapped her phone screen, but to her surprise, the app showed no available rides.

"What's going on?" Emma muttered, frowning as she checked again. "The cabs aren't booking. It looks like they're all unavailable."

She turned to Rolex, explaining the situation with a slight sigh. "I guess we’ll have to wait a bit longer."

Mouni, who had been quietly listening to the exchange, perked up at the opportunity. She glanced up at her dad with hopeful eyes. "Dad, can Emma stay with us tonight? Please, I really want her to stay!"

Rolex looked at Mouni, a small frown crossing his face. "Mouni, it's not really polite to ask someone to stay over like that," he said gently, trying to explain the boundaries.

Mouni’s face fell a little, but then she added, "But Dad, please? She can stay until we reach home. It is not far, and then we can drop her off. Just for tonight."

Rolex considered this for a moment. He knew Mouni was only thinking of making Emma feel welcome, and she truly liked her new friend. He sighed, realizing there was not much harm in it for a short time.

"Alright," Rolex finally said, looking at Emma. "She’s right. You can come with us until we reach our place, and we will drop you off after that. But just for tonight."

Emma, who had been listening to the back-and-forth, smiled warmly at Mouni. "Thanks for the invitation, Mouni. I’d love to come along with you guys."

Mouni grinned, excited that her request had been accepted. "Yay! Thank you, Emma!" She then turned to Rolex, her eyes bright. "Let’s go, Dad!"

And with that, they began walking toward the car. Mouni, holding tightly onto her father’s hand, felt a deep sense of happiness, knowing that she was creating new memories with the people she cared about. Emma was now a part of their lives, and she could not wait to share more moments with her in the future.

As they walked down the darkened streets, the night air was thick with an uneasy stillness. The usual sounds of the city were dimmed, and the only light came from flickering street lamps, casting long, twisted shadows across the pavement. Mouni clung tightly to her father’s hand, and Emma walked close by, her eyes scanning the surroundings as they made their way home. It was late, and the streets felt emptier than usual.

Suddenly, the group stopped as a harsh voice called out from the shadows. A gang of thieves emerged from the alleyway, their eyes glinting in the darkness. One of them stepped forward, brandishing a knife, its blade catching the light as he waved it menacingly toward them.

"Give us your valuables, all of you!" the leader growled, his voice rough with desperation.

Emma froze, her heart racing as the situation unfolded. Mouni clutched her father’s hand even tighter, sensing the danger. But Rolex stood still, his expression calm, his eyes narrowing slightly.

In an instant, Rolex closed his eyes, and a flood of memories surged through his mind. The past he had tried to forget. He remembered the countless times he had faced similar threats—times when his strength and cold demeanor had saved him. He had killed before, fought countless battles, and been in situations far more perilous. Yet, tonight was different. He was with Mouni and Emma. His mind raced back to his years of training, the darkness he had embraced to survive.

He opened his eyes slowly and locked them on the leader of the gang. Mouni, sensing something was wrong, looked up at her father with worry, while Emma stepped closer to him, her breath caught in her throat.

Before anyone could react, the thief noticed Rolex’s watch. The gleaming timepiece, rich and unmistakable, caught the thief’s attention, and his eyes widened with recognition. The thief immediately stopped his forward movement, now eyeing Rolex with a mix of fear and awe.

"Wait, wait," the leader said, raising his hand to halt the others. "Don’t hurt them. Look at his watch."

He turned to his gang members, barking orders in a hushed tone. “This is not just anyone. Let them go.”

The other thieves hesitated, their knives still held out, but they stood down as their leader spoke again. "Sorry, sir," he muttered, backing away slowly, eyes still on Rolex. “You can go. We do not want any trouble with someone like you.”

Rolex kept his gaze steady on them, but just as they were about to move on, the leader spoke up once more.

"Wait," the man said, his voice quieter now, laced with uncertainty. "I don’t know who you are, but I can sense you’re not like others. You are... different. I am sorry for the trouble I caused. But I need to ask a favor, and I hope you will help me. Please... I need your watch."

For a moment, the street was silent. Mouni stared at the thief in confusion, but Rolex’s eyes were locked onto the man’s face, reading his desperation. There was a moment of tension in the air, a quiet understanding that no words needed to be spoken.

Without hesitation, Rolex reached down and removed his watch, holding it out to the leader. His gaze remained steady, his voice cold but firm as he spoke.

"I don’t want to see you doing this again," Rolex said quietly, his words carrying a weight of finality. “Take it, but change your path. Do not make the same mistake again.”

The leader took the watch, his hands shaking as he quickly tucked it into his pocket. His gaze lingered on Rolex for a second longer, as if considering something, before he nodded stiffly. “Thank you... I will not forget this.”

Rolex turned away, motioning for Mouni and Emma to follow him. He did not look back, and the group continued walking away from the scene. Mouni, who had been silent through the whole exchange, tugged at her father’s sleeve.

"Dad," she said in a small voice, "why did you give him the watch?"

Rolex glanced down at her and smiled softly, though there was a hint of sadness in his eyes. "Sometimes, it’s not about the watch, Mouni. It is about showing mercy. You never know when someone’s life can change because of a single act of kindness."

Emma, who had been quietly observing, nodded in understanding. She did not fully grasp the magnitude of what had just happened, but she knew that Rolex was not just any ordinary man.

As they walked farther down the street, the lights of the city shone brighter ahead, and the weight of the night’s encounter seemed to lift, though it lingered in the back of their minds. They were safe—this time. But for Rolex, this was just another reminder of the dark past he could never escape.

As Rolex, Mouni, and Emma walked away, the sound of the gang retreating faded behind them. However, not all of the thieves were so quick to accept the situation.

One of the younger thieves, clearly unsettled, turned to the leader. His voice trembled as he asked, "Why did you let him go? He had everything... he is rich, and we could have had it all!"

The leader, still holding the watch Rolex had given him, took a deep breath and let out a sigh. His hands were shaking slightly from the adrenaline, but his eyes reflected something deeper—fear.

"I know exactly who he is," the leader muttered, his voice low, almost reverential. "If we didn’t let them go right now, tomorrow we’d all be dead. You do not mess with people like him. That man... he is not just some rich guy. He's someone who could end us all with a single word."

The younger thief frowned, still struggling to understand. "But... the watch, it’s worth so much! Why give it up so easily?"

The leader looked down at the gleaming timepiece in his hand, his expression serious. "This watch is more valuable than any of you realize. It is worth more than all the stuff we could steal in a year. I know what I’m holding here—this could sell for almost three million pounds."

The younger thief's eyes widened in disbelief. "Three million?! That is enough to set us up for life!"

"Exactly," the leader replied, his voice now filled with determination. "If we sell this watch, we could live comfortably for the rest of our lives. No more thefts, no more risks, no more hiding in the shadows. We could have a real future. But..." He paused, looking at his gang with a hard expression, "the price of that future is making sure we never do something like this again."

The other thieves were silent for a moment, clearly processing what their leader had said. The idea of never having to steal again—living freely—seemed to hit them hard. The desperation that had led them to this point suddenly seemed distant. Perhaps there was another way.

"From now on, we don’t steal. We sell this watch, get ourselves out of this life. No more hurting people," the leader continued, his voice firm and resolute. "We owe that man—whatever he is—to leave this path behind. He gave us a chance. Let's not waste it."

The gang members nodded slowly, some of them still unsure, but the sense of relief began to settle in. The leader’s words carried a weight of truth, and as they looked at the watch again, they understood that this was their turning point. No longer would they resort to the dangerous, fleeting thrills of theft. They could change. They would change.

With a final glance at the group, the leader tucked the watch safely into his pocket. "Let's go," he ordered. "We’ve got a future to plan."
