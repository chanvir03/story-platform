Chapter 1: Bajarangi and Suvarchala

Bajarangi and Suvarchala

The sun dipped low in the sky, casting a warm golden glow over the village of Kishkinda Colony, nestled in the heart of Kadapa. The entire village was alive with celebration. Hanuman Jayanthi, the birthday of Lord Hanuman, was being celebrated with immense fervor. The streets were adorned with saffron flags, banners, and cloths, fluttering in the warm breeze. The colour of devotion and strength — saffron — filled every corner, from the towering trees to the small, humble homes.

Everywhere, the sounds of the Hanuman Chalisa rang out, as men, women, and children sang in unison, their voices filled with love and reverence for the mighty Lord Hanuman. The rhythm of their chanting blended seamlessly with the occasional dhol (drum) beat and the reverberating echoes of conch shells, creating an atmosphere that felt as if the very air was charged with divine energy. The villagers were in a trance, lost in their devotion, as they celebrated the strength and courage that Lord Hanuman represented.

But in the midst of the festival, there was a more intimate, personal story unfolding.

Inside a small house at the edge of the village, Anjali, a woman of grace and warmth, lay in the grip of labour pains. Her body trembled with each contraction, and her face was pale with the effort. She gripped the edges of the old wooden bed, her knuckles white as she fought to steady her breathing. Her mind was flooded with both joy and fear — joy at the thought of the child she was about to bring into the world, but fear of the unknown, of what the future held for this child.

In the dim light of the room, an elderly woman, Lalithambika, Anjali’s mother-in-law, moved about quickly, gathering things together. Her movements were frantic but purposeful. The old woman muttered soothing words, but there was an undeniable sense of urgency in her voice. "Anjali, stay calm, dear. It won’t be long now," she said, placing a cool cloth on Anjali’s forehead.

Anjali’s breathing quickened. "Mother," she gasped between waves of pain, "It’s time... it’s really time."

Lalithambika’s face filled with both concern and excitement. "Yes, yes, it’s time for our little one to arrive. I’ll call for the ambulance. Stay strong, my daughter."

With trembling hands, the old woman rushed outside, her voice carrying into the night as she called for help. "The baby’s coming! Get the ambulance!" The urgency in her voice pierced through the sounds of the distant festival, and soon enough, the sound of an ambulance siren echoed down the narrow lanes of the village.

Inside the house, Anjali lay back on the bed, her body aching from the labour. She squeezed her eyes shut, trying to concentrate on her breathing. Her mind was spinning. "What if the child is born and the world doesn’t accept him? What if he’s not liked the other children?" These thoughts raced through her mind, even as she struggled against the pain.

Anjali’s husband, Vata Deva, was performing his evening prayers at the village temple, as he did every day. A man of unwavering faith, he sat in silent devotion, his mind focused on the divine energy of Hanuman, whose blessings he sought for both himself and his family. The air around him seemed charged with the same intensity as the voices of the villagers chanting the Hanuman Chalisa. He could feel the energy of the festival, but something was off. His heart raced suddenly, and an uneasy feeling crept over him.

The phone in his pocket buzzed, pulling him from his prayer. It was his mother’s voice on the other end. "Vata! Anjali is in labour! You must come quickly to the hospital!" Her voice trembled with worry.

Vata Deva’s heart skipped a beat. His hands tightened around the phone, and without a word, he stood up, his prayer unfinished. His thoughts rushed to Anjali. "She’s strong. She’ll be fine," he muttered to himself, though his heart was heavy with concern. He didn’t waste a moment longer, quickly leaving the temple behind, his feet moving with purpose as he headed toward the Anjanamma Hospital.

The streets, which had been alive with the sounds of celebration just moments ago, now seemed distant as he raced through them, his mind focused entirely on his wife. The hospital was just on the edge of the village, and soon, Vata Deva arrived. He stepped inside, the cool air of the hospital a sharp contrast to the warmth of the evening outside.

As he entered the hospital, he saw Lalithambika, his mother, standing by the entrance, looking as if she had been waiting for him. Her face was a mix of joy and anxiety. She didn’t waste time in exchanging pleasantries, her voice urgent. "Vata, Anjali has just given birth. A baby boy. Come, come quickly!"

Vata Deva’s breath caught in his throat. A baby boy? A wave of emotion flooded him, but he steadied himself. He needed to be strong for Anjali. He nodded and followed her through the sterile halls of the hospital.

The doctor emerged from the operation theatre, his expression calm. "Congratulations, Vata Deva," the doctor said. "You’ve been blessed with a son."

The news sent a surge of pride through Vata Deva’s chest. His eyes sparkled, and a wide smile broke across his face. "A son," he whispered, as he rushed toward Anjali’s room.

Anjali lay on the bed, looking both tired and relieved. Her face softened when she saw Vata Deva enter. He knelt beside her and gently took her hand, pressing it to his chest. "You’ve done it, Anjali," he said softly, his voice filled with tenderness. "You’ve brought our son into the world."

Anjali, too exhausted to speak, smiled faintly, her lips not fully parting. There was a deep understanding in her eyes, a silent exchange that spoke volumes. She didn’t need words — her love for him and their child was clear.

Vata Deva reached forward, brushing a strand of hair from her forehead. His lips touched her brow, a kiss of gratitude, of love, of protection. "Your turn is over," he whispered. "Now, it’s my turn to take care of him. We’ll protect him together."

Anjali’s eyes fluttered shut for a moment, and she nodded, resting her head against Vata Deva’s chest. The room, filled with the sterile scent of the hospital, seemed to fade away. All that mattered was the moment they shared, the new life they had created.

Later that night, after the hospital visit and the celebrations had settled down, Anjali and Vata Deva returned home. The house, though humble, was filled with warmth. Anjali sat by the cradle, looking at her newborn son, who was peacefully sleeping. His tiny form swaddled in soft white cloth, his face serene and angelic.

But then, Anjali’s eyes fell upon something that made her freeze — a tail.

At first, she thought it was some kind of illusion, some strange trick of the light, but no. There, nestled against her son’s small back, was a distinct and soft tail.

Anjali’s heart raced. She gently touched the tail, feeling its softness beneath her fingertips. Her thoughts spun wildly. "What is this? Why does he have a tail?"

Her breath was shallow as she turned to Vata Deva, who was standing by the door, watching her with a mixture of curiosity and calm.

"Vata… look," she said, her voice trembling. "Our son… He has a tail."

Vata Deva approached slowly, his gaze falling upon the small tail. He smiled, his eyes filled with pride. "It’s a gift, Anjali. A blessing," he said softly. "Our son is marked by the divine. He is Bajarangi — born on the day of Hanuman Jayanthi. This tail is a symbol of strength, of power."

Anjali, still concerned, furrowed her brow. "But what if the world doesn’t accept him? What if they call him a monkey man?"

Vata Deva knelt beside her, his expression full of love and reassurance. "Let the world say what they will. The child is destined for greatness. He has the strength of Hanuman in him. A tail is nothing to fear. It is a mark of his divine power."

Anjali looked down at their son, her heart swelling with love, yet tinged with a hint of worry. She took a deep breath. "We will protect him. We will make sure the world knows the truth."

Vata Deva kissed her forehead gently. "Yes, Anjali. And one day, when he grows, the world will know his name. Bajarangi."

The name echoed in the stillness of the night, a name that would carry power, strength, and destiny.

The River Penna flowed gently under the bright afternoon sun, its waters glistening as the breeze whispered through the trees. The banks were a peaceful refuge, far from the busy world, and it was here that Surya Narayan and his wife, Lalithambika, had decided to spend the day. Lalithambika, her pregnancy nearing its final stage, looked out over the river with a sense of calm, while Surya, ever protective, sat beside her, his arm around her shoulders as they enjoyed the quiet beauty of nature.

Surya smiled at Lalithambika, his heart filled with love for her and the life they were about to bring into the world. They had dreamed of this moment for months — the arrival of their first child. Surya had always wanted to be a father, and now, that dream was about to become a reality.

As they sat there in the peace of the moment, Lalithambika suddenly winced, a sharp pain seizing her body. Surya’s eyes snapped to her in alarm.

"Lalitha?" he asked, his voice full of concern. "What is it?"

Lalithambika gritted her teeth, her hand clutching Surya’s as the pain intensified. "It’s… it’s time, Surya," she gasped, struggling to keep her voice steady. "The baby… is coming."

Surya’s heart raced. They were far from the village, with no access to medical help nearby. Panic gripped him as he looked around — the riverbank was desolate, and there was no sign of anyone who could help.

"Lalitha, stay calm. We’ll get help," Surya said, though his voice was shaking. He tried to steady his breath as he glanced desperately around. But it seemed there was no one nearby to help, and the hospital was too far for them to reach in time.

Then, like a beacon of hope, Surya spotted a small group of washerwomen down by the river, their hands busy scrubbing clothes. Without wasting a second, Surya ran to them, his face a mask of anxiety.

"My wife is in labor!" he shouted. "Please, someone help me! The baby is coming, and we’re too far from the hospital."

One of the women, a calm and steady figure named Rukmini, stepped forward. "We will help you," she said, her voice firm. "Bring your wife to us."

Surya quickly returned to Lalithambika, who was now lying on the ground, her face flushed with pain. With Rukmini’s assistance, the women guided Surya and Lalithambika to a patch of soft grass beside the river. The women arranged cloths and gave Lalithambika the support she needed, making sure she was as comfortable as possible.

Surya, though frightened and unprepared for the birth, stayed by her side. He held her hand tightly, whispering calming words. "Lalitha, just breathe. I’m here with you. You’re so strong. Our child will be here soon."

The hours stretched on, and the labor intensified. Surya’s chest tightened as he watched Lalithambika endure each wave of pain. He wiped the sweat from her forehead, his own hands trembling. His mind raced, filled with worry, but he remained focused on her, offering all the love and strength he had to give.

Through the pain, Lalithambika held on, her breaths shallow but determined. Surya was beside her, never leaving her side, holding her hand, and offering his silent strength. Then, after what seemed like an eternity, the air was filled with the sound of a baby’s cry. Surya’s heart soared, and tears filled his eyes.

"It’s over, Lalitha," Surya whispered, his voice thick with emotion. "Our baby is here."

Rukmini, who had assisted with the birth, looked up at Surya and smiled. "It’s a girl," she said gently, handing him their newborn daughter, wrapped in a cloth.

Surya’s hands shook as he took the tiny baby into his arms. His heart swelled with love as he gazed down at his daughter. She was so small, so delicate, her tiny fingers curling around his. Surya couldn’t believe it — their daughter, their precious little girl, was finally here.

Lalithambika, exhausted but radiant, gazed up at Surya, a tired but loving smile on her lips. "Surya… our daughter," she whispered, her voice filled with joy and relief.

Surya leaned down and kissed her forehead. "Thank you, Lalitha," he said, his voice thick with emotion. "You were so brave. I don’t know how you did it."

Lalithambika, too tired to say much, gently placed her index finger on Surya’s lips, signaling him to stop talking. Her smile said everything. She was too exhausted to speak, but her gesture was full of understanding. Surya knew she had given everything to bring their child into the world.

"I love you, Lalitha," Surya whispered, his voice full of promise. "I love both of you more than words can say."

Lalithambika smiled softly, her eyelids fluttering as she rested. Surya, not wanting to disturb her, gazed down at their daughter, feeling an overwhelming sense of peace. Holding her in his arms, he felt a bond that was deeper than anything he had ever known.

The washerwomen, who had helped deliver the baby, smiled at the family. "She’s a blessing," Rukmini said, her voice full of warmth. "May she bring happiness to your home."

Surya nodded, his heart overflowing with gratitude. "Thank you," he said to the women. "I don’t know how we would have made it through without your help."

As the sun began to set, casting a golden glow across the river, Surya gently lifted Lalithambika into his arms. He held their daughter close, her tiny body nestled against his chest. Together, they made their way back to the village, the journey back filled with a deep sense of awe and fulfillment.

Surya whispered softly to Lalithambika, "I’ll always protect you both. I’ll always be here."

The river flowed beside them, a steady companion, as they walked into their new life with their precious daughter.

In the warm, bustling home of Surya Narayan and Lalithambika, a festive atmosphere filled the air. Family members, neighbors, and friends had gathered to celebrate the joyous occasion of the birth of Surya’s daughter. The house was full of love and laughter, as people admired the newborn girl, whose arrival had been long awaited.

As they all marveled at the little girl in Lalithambika's arms, one of the guests asked, "What have you decided to name her, Surya?"

Lalithambika, holding her daughter close, smiled warmly and answered with pride, "Her name will be Suvarchala, which means 'Radiant as the Sun,' for she will bring light and strength to our lives, just as the sun lights up the world."

The room filled with approving murmurs and smiles, but one person, a relative, smiled kindly and added, "Matha ji, please speak in our language. The name sounds beautiful, but we would love to hear it in our own tongue."

Lalithambika, understanding their request, replied in the local dialect with a gentle laugh. "Her name, dear friends, is Suvarchala."

As the evening continued with joy and excitement, there was an undercurrent of tension, for news had begun to spread about a new challenge looming on the horizon.

The Challenge

At that moment, in the shadow of the festive atmosphere, an unexpected confrontation began to take shape. Rahu, a fierce and infamous warrior, known for his ruthless skills in Matti Yudham, the ancient martial art, had heard of Surya’s recent success and strength. Rahu, filled with pride and a thirst for glory, decided it was time to challenge Surya.

Sitting across from Soma, Surya’s trusted friend, Rahu's voice was filled with disdain. "Soma, I want to face your friend Surya in a battle. I challenge him to a fight in the Matti Yudham. If he dares to accept, let him know that I’ll defeat him easily."

Soma, a loyal friend to Surya, remained calm, though he felt the tension rising. "Rahu, now is not the time," Soma said, his voice firm. "Surya has just had a daughter today. This is a time for celebration, not for fighting."

But Rahu wasn’t one to back down easily. He sneered, "Celebration or not, if your friend is a true warrior, he won’t shy away from my challenge."

Soma glanced at Rahu, his patience wearing thin. "Rahu, I said he is busy with his family. We’re celebrating the birth of his daughter. You can come back another time if you truly want to prove yourself."

But Rahu, undeterred by Soma’s words, growled, "I’m not asking for your permission. I’m telling you that if Surya is a real warrior, he will accept my challenge. Tell him to be ready. I will give him a day to prepare for his defeat."

The challenge was set. Surya’s reputation as a warrior was now in danger, and Rahu’s confidence in his own skills was unwavering.

Back at Surya’s home, the joyous celebration continued, and Surya spent precious moments with his newborn daughter, Suvarchala. Surya’s heart swelled with love for her, feeling a deep connection to the tiny life in his arms. The world outside seemed far away in these moments of bliss.

However, Soma soon arrived with troubling news. "Surya," Soma said, his face serious. "Rahu has issued a challenge. He wants to face you in a fight. He believes you will fall before him in battle. He doesn’t care about your celebration. He insists on fighting you tomorrow."

Surya, though caught off guard by the challenge, remained calm. He looked down at his daughter, whose peaceful face filled him with a renewed sense of determination. "I will face Rahu," Surya said, his voice steady. "I won’t back down. This is what I’ve been trained for — to protect my honor, my family, and my people."

Soma nodded, though concern flickered in his eyes. "Surya, Rahu is a fierce opponent. He has been training for years, and his skill in Matti Yudham is unmatched. You should be careful."

But Surya, ever confident in his abilities, smiled. "Let him come. This battle is not just for me. It’s for my daughter, for my family, and for all those who have trusted me with their faith."

Later that evening, as the sun set over the village, Surya stood alone by the window, looking out over the land he had fought to protect. Lalithambika, who had been busy with the guests, now joined him. Her eyes were soft with concern, but she said nothing, simply resting her hand on his shoulder.

"I heard about Rahu’s challenge," Lalithambika said quietly. "Are you sure you want to go through with this, Surya?"

Surya turned to her, his face warm with resolve. "I must, Lalitha. He threatens our peace, and I cannot let him think he can intimidate me. But don’t worry, I will return. I promise you that."

Lalithambika looked at him, her gaze filled with both pride and worry. She kissed his cheek softly. "Just be careful, Surya. You are our strength, and we need you. Our daughter needs you."

Surya took her hands in his, his voice filled with love. "I will fight, and I will win. For you, for Suvarchala, and for everyone who believes in me."

With that, Surya turned to prepare for the battle ahead. The night before the fight was filled with restless anticipation. Soma came to check on him one last time. "Are you ready, Surya?"

Surya, with calm determination in his eyes, nodded. "Tomorrow, the world will witness the glory of my battle. Let Rahu prepare himself. I’m ready."

Chapter 2: Shadows of Ambition

Shadows of Ambition

Meanwhile, across the village, Rahu was deep in practice. He worked with relentless determination, his every move precise and calculated. He knew that Surya was a formidable fighter, but Rahu was convinced that he could defeat him. With every strike, every maneuver, Rahu trained with a singular focus — victory would be his.

The morning sun had barely risen, and the village was already buzzing with excitement. Word of the grand battle had spread quickly, and people from surrounding areas had gathered to witness the showdown between Surya Narayan, the renowned warrior of Matti Yudham, and Rahu, the formidable challenger.

At the arena, the air was thick with tension. The mat, marked by the deep red soil, was set for the epic contest. Surya stood on one side, his eyes focused, his muscles coiled with the readiness of a seasoned fighter. His thoughts, however, were not on the battle alone — he had his wife, Lalithambika, and his newborn daughter, Suvarchala, in his heart. This was not just a fight for his honor, but for their future.

On the other side stood Rahu, his expression cold and filled with ego. His body was like a rock, prepared and trained for this moment, but his overconfidence would soon become his greatest flaw. His master, Azad, stood near him, his eyes narrowing as he observed the situation.

"Rahu," Azad said with a serious tone, "you made a big mistake. You should have taken more time to prepare. You are not ready yet. This fight will be difficult."

Rahu’s eyes flashed with arrogance, and he scoffed at Azad's words. "I know when it’s time to fight," Rahu said, his voice firm and proud. "You’re just my master, but no one can tell me anything about my defeat. I don’t listen to anyone. I will defeat him, and no one can defeat me."

Azad looked at Rahu, shaking his head slightly. "Hubris will be your downfall," he muttered, but Rahu wasn’t listening.

Meanwhile, the crowd around the arena grew in anticipation. The referee, an experienced elder from the village, stepped forward and announced the beginning of the battle.

"The rules of Matti Yudham are simple," he said, his voice carrying across the crowd. "The fight will be fought on this mat filled with red soil. The first person who steps off the mat or whose head touches the ground first will be declared defeated."

With those words, the crowd fell silent, and the two combatants, Surya and Rahu, took their positions. The tension was palpable. Every spectator knew that this was more than just a physical contest; it was a battle for pride, for glory, and for the legacy of both fighters.

The match began with a loud signal, and the two warriors collided with a force that could be felt in the air. Rahu immediately took the offensive, charging at Surya with raw power, throwing punches and kicks, each blow seeming to carry the weight of his ego. Surya, ever calm and calculated, blocked and parried, moving with fluid precision. His movements were graceful but lethal, honed through years of training in Matti Yudham.

The crowd roared with excitement as the two men moved across the mat, their bodies locked in a fierce struggle. For a moment, it seemed that Surya had the upper hand — his experience and skill were evident in every move. But Rahu, with his overwhelming strength and relentless aggression, quickly changed the tide of the battle.

In the blink of an eye, Rahu managed to land a crushing blow on Surya’s shoulder, sending him stumbling back. The crowd gasped, unsure of what would happen next. Rahu’s confidence surged as he pressed forward, eager to finish the fight.

Surya, however, didn’t falter. Despite the pain, he regained his footing and continued to defend himself with precision. The fight was becoming more intense, each moment more unpredictable than the last. At first, it seemed like a one-sided battle, but now, Rahu’s actions had created doubt in everyone’s mind. The question was no longer about whether Surya would win, but how he would manage to overcome Rahu’s brute strength.

The battle raged on, neither fighter willing to give an inch. The mat was now covered in dust and dirt from the fierce struggle, but the warriors remained determined. Surya’s mind was focused not just on the battle but on the promise he had made to his family — to return victorious, to protect the future of his daughter, Suvarchala.

Finally, after a series of rapid exchanges, Surya saw his opening. Rahu, growing increasingly tired and overconfident, left himself vulnerable. Surya, seizing the moment, executed a flawless counter-attack. With a swift movement, he used his opponent's momentum against him, sending Rahu stumbling toward the edge of the mat. The crowd gasped in shock as Rahu fought to regain his balance, but it was too late. His foot stepped off the mat, and his head collided with the ground.

The referee’s voice rang out across the arena. "Rahu is defeated! Surya Narayan is the winner!"

The crowd erupted into cheers, their excitement reaching a fever pitch as Surya stood tall, victorious. Despite the grueling battle, Surya’s face remained calm and composed. He had won not through brute strength but through skill, patience, and experience.

Surya turned to Rahu, who was slowly rising from the ground, his ego bruised but not broken. Rahu’s pride had taken a hit, but Surya’s respect for him grew. In the heat of the battle, he had realized that Rahu was a worthy opponent.

"You’ve changed my thoughts, Rahu," Surya said, his voice sincere. "I once believed that I was the only one who could defeat me, but today, you’ve proven to me that there are others who are worthy of respect. From now on, you are my greatest opponent."

Rahu didn’t respond immediately. He stood there, silent for a moment, before nodding curtly. His face, though marked by defeat, was filled with a grudging respect for Surya. He had underestimated him, but he now understood the true strength of his opponent.

Without a word, Rahu turned and walked away, his pride wounded but his spirit unbroken. Surya watched him go, knowing that this battle had not only proven his own strength but had earned him the respect of his rival.

As Surya left the arena, the cheers of the crowd still echoing in his ears, he thought about the battle, the lessons it had taught him, and what lay ahead. His victory wasn’t just a personal triumph; it was a promise to his family, to his daughter, Suvarchala, that he would always protect them and fight for their future.

Later that evening, Surya returned home to his family. Lalithambika smiled warmly as she held Suvarchala in her arms. Surya knelt beside her, gently touching his daughter’s tiny hand.

"Today, I fought for you, Suvarchala," Surya whispered, his heart full of love and pride. "And I will always fight for you, no matter what."

Rahu's anger consumed him. His body was trembling with frustration and humiliation after his defeat at the hands of Surya Narayan. He stood in the middle of his destroyed house, glaring at the broken remnants of his possessions. His fists clenched, the reality of his loss weighing heavily on him. He was 32, a warrior who had earned a reputation, but a 26-year-old man, Surya, had humiliated him in front of the entire village. It was unthinkable. It was shameful.

"Shit! I am 32, but I was just defeated by a 26-year-old man!" Rahu muttered, his voice dripping with rage. "It was a disgrace to be beaten by him... A young man who’s barely tasted the bitterness of true power."

Unable to contain his anger, Rahu stormed through his house, overturning furniture, smashing vases, and tearing apart anything in his path. His heart burned with a desire for revenge, but deep inside, a seed of doubt began to form. He knew that strength alone would not be enough to defeat Surya — he needed something greater.

In his fury, Rahu retreated to his study room. The walls were lined with books, ancient texts, and scrolls that spoke of warriors, gods, and the secrets of the universe. His eyes fell on an old, worn-out copy of the Mahabharata, the epic Hindu text. The book had always intrigued him — filled with tales of gods, demons, and legendary battles. Rahu had read parts of it before, but today, it felt like the key to something far more significant.

He picked up the book, his hands trembling with an intensity he had never felt before. As he flipped through the pages, he began to lose himself in the stories of Lord Krishna, Arjuna, and the divine weapons that had shaped the course of history. The more he read, the more he began to believe in the idea that the gods were not just myths or stories — they were real, and their power could be harnessed.

Suddenly, Rahu slammed the book shut, his mind racing with an epiphany.

"If the Mahabharata is real, then the gods must be real too," Rahu thought aloud. "And if they exist, then surely they can be called upon. If we perform tapas — the sacred worship of gods — we can receive their blessings and grow stronger. The gods will appear before us, and we will gain their boons."

His heart pounded with newfound purpose. He had read about the ancient wonders of Dwaraka and the Ramasethu (the bridge built by Lord Rama). The stories spoke of miracles and divine intervention that had stood the test of time, lasting for millions of years. If these things could endure, then what was stopping him from acquiring the same power? The gods had granted strength to warriors like Arjuna, so why not to him?

"I will do it," Rahu declared, his eyes burning with determination. "I will perform tapas. I will call upon the gods. I will seek their boons, and when I do, I will become unstoppable. I will rule the world. The earth will bow to my knees."

His voice echoed through the empty room, vibrating with the weight of his conviction. Rahu was no longer just a defeated warrior; he had transformed into something far more dangerous. The thirst for power had taken over him entirely. The pain of his defeat had turned into a driving force that would push him beyond his limits.

"I am Rahubali," he said, his tone filled with raw ambition. "And I will bring the world under my control."

With that, Rahu set his mind on his next course of action: a path that would lead him to the gods themselves, to gain the strength he so desperately craved.

The night was still and dark as Rahu packed his belongings, preparing for a journey that would change the course of his life. He didn’t tell anyone where he was going, not even his master Azad. Rahu’s mind was made up — he would travel to the Himalayan ranges, the sacred land where many great warriors had gone to perform tapas, a rigorous spiritual practice to gain power from the gods. There, he would perform the deepest form of meditation and sacrifice, hoping to earn the divine boons he so desperately desired.

With his bag packed and his resolve firm, Rahu started his journey. He rode through the dark, empty roads on his bike, the cold wind whipping against his face, but his heart burned with a new intensity. His defeat had pushed him into a corner, and now, he was determined to come out stronger, far stronger than ever before. He would return as a new man, a warrior worthy of ruling the world.

As Rahu journeyed through the dense forest roads, he came across a disturbing sight. A man was lying on the road, clearly drunk, unable to stand, and in a state of complete disarray. The man was murmuring incoherently, his body swaying with no control. Rahu’s eyes narrowed as he approached.

At that moment, something dark and twisted stirred within him. His anger and frustration from his defeat, his sense of humiliation, took over. In a cold, calculated move, Rahu decided to eliminate the drunken man.

With a swift motion, Rahu ended the man’s life. It wasn’t out of necessity, but because he needed to prove to himself that no one, not even the weakest of men, could challenge him. The darkness within him grew as he watched the life fade from the man’s eyes. He didn’t think twice.

In a matter of moments, Rahu set the man’s body on fire along with his bike, ensuring there would be no evidence left. He placed the man’s identity card near the burning remains, a malicious plan taking root in his mind.

"This will make sure no one finds out that I’m still alive," Rahu thought to himself as he looked at the flames licking the sky. "Everyone will believe that Rahubali is dead."

The next morning, the authorities arrived at the crime scene. They found the charred body and the half-burnt identity card. When the police officer examined the card, his eyes widened in recognition. The identity card belonged to none other than Rahu — the infamous warrior of Matti Yudham.

Lord Shiva, gazing deeply into Rahu’s eyes, saw the purity of his determination. He nodded slowly, understanding the depth of Rahu’s request, and with a divine gesture, granted him the boon.
